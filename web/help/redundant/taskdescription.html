The MovementVR Task

The tasks designed with MovementVR are lifting tasks. On each trial, participants lift a plate of grapes up to a target with a bird perched on it. Their hands are tracked in real time and rendered as fully articulated virtual hands that interact with objects just like in real life. This allows participants to lift the virtual plate in the same way they would in the real world.

Task Elements
  • Plate & Grape: The object participants lift each trial. Physics-based interaction allows natural grasp and movement. If it tilts or falls, the grape drops.
  • Target: The goal location for the plate. It can change color to indicate success/failure and includes a visible mesh that defines feedback and trial conditions.
  • Hand Home: Starting position for each hand, defined by location and a "no-hand" zone to avoid premature interaction.
  • Bird & Perch: An animated bird sits at the target. Its species, sound, and behavior provide outcome-linked feedback.
  • Feedback Messages: Cartoons and sounds show success or failure after each trial.
  • Hands: Real-time tracked, articulated VR hands with 19 bones for natural interaction.
  • Workspace: The bounded area within which the plate must stay. Exiting ends the trial as a failure.
  • Text & UI: Instructional messages, trial number, and visual elements support user guidance.
  • Background: A medieval-style immersive garden and port setting with ambient birds and scenery.
Trial Phases
  • Intertrial: Pause between trials.
  • Home Position: Hands return to defined start zone.
  • Plate Contact: User initiates lift by touching the plate.
  • Plate Lift: Active lifting phase toward the target.
  • Feedback: Outcome shown via color, animation, or cartoon.
  • Break: Optional rest between trials.
Success and Failure Criteria
  • Success: Plate center reaches target zone, remains within for required duration, and stays below tilt threshold.
  • Failure: Plate tilts or leaves zone too soon, falls, exits workspace, or exceeds time limit.
Motor Learning Applications
  • Adaptation via Gain Perturbation: Virtual hand moves with scaled gain relative to real hand. Perturbation ramps up and down by phase.
  • Reinforcement Learning: Real-time visual feedback plus adaptive animations and sounds tied to success or failure.
  • Custom Feedback: Target color, bird behavior, and effects can change based on task outcome.
