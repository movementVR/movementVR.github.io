Features
Realism
  • Full 3D motion: Move freely in space without artificial constraints. No 2D or limited-joint movements.
  • Realistic environment: Immerse users in a naturalistic 3D medieval garden with trees, a port, and birds that fly and chirp. No blank workspaces or basic shapes.
  • Natural hands and object manipulation: Use your own hands — tracked and articulated in real time in VR — to grasp and interact with objects just like in real life. Objects respond naturally, according to real-world physics. No controllers or single-point-effectors.
  • Real-life task dynamics: Engage participants in the everyday action of lifting a plate, with natural redundancy in movements and outcomes. There isn’t a single “correct” hand position — multiple grips, paths, and strategies can succeed. But nothing’s guaranteed: the plate can still tilt, the grape can slip, or the whole thing can fall. No rigid rules where a single cursor position defines success.
  • Common and clinically relevant: Study the kinds of movements people actually use in daily life — both unimanual and bimanual — and the tasks that support rehabilitation, like adaptation to gain-based perturbations for error-augmentation training. No learning of abstract relationships like reaching at arbitrary angles.

Accessibility
  • Standalone and affordable: Run experiments and record kinematic data directly on VR headsets — no external computer or motion tracking equipment needed.
  • No coding required: User‑friendly interface designed for researchers and clinicians.
  • Highly customizable: Over 500 editable parameters, including support for motor adaptation and reinforcement learning.
